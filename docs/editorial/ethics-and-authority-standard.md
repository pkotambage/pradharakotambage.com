# Ethics and Authority Standard

This document governs trust, authorship and professional-identity signals on pradharakotambage.com.

## 1. Ethical baseline

The website is a legal knowledge and legal-literacy platform. Professional credibility should be established through accurate identification, transparent authorship, careful sourcing and restrained presentation.

Do not use:
- testimonials or client endorsements;
- comparative claims such as "best", "leading", "top", "expert" or "specialist" unless a legally recognised status objectively justifies the wording;
- success rates, win rates or outcome guarantees;
- language implying that a reader should retain the author;
- urgency or fear-based calls to contact the author;
- inducements, discounts or promotional offers;
- descriptions of client matters that could compromise confidentiality;
- wording designed to unfairly attract clientele.

## 2. Permitted trust signals

Use objective, verifiable information such as:
- name and professional title;
- admission as an Attorney-at-Law;
- academic qualifications;
- factual description of areas in which professional work has principally been undertaken;
- authorship of articles;
- publication dates;
- legal sources and statutory references;
- clear distinctions between law, procedure, practical observation and educational commentary;
- neutral disclaimers explaining that material is general information.

## 3. Author description

### Legal Guides
Default wording:

> Pradhara Kotambage is an Attorney-at-Law in Sri Lanka whose professional work principally involves civil and commercial disputes, litigation and court practice. These Legal Guides are written for general legal literacy.

### Dispute Resolution
Default wording:

> Pradhara Kotambage is an Attorney-at-Law in Sri Lanka with professional experience in civil and commercial disputes and a developing professional focus on mediation and constructive dispute resolution.

The wording should remain factual and should not imply accreditation, specialisation or professional status that has not yet been obtained.

## 4. Dates

- Show the publication date where known.
- Add an "Updated" or "Reviewed" date only where the article has actually been substantively reviewed or revised.
- Do not use a technical file-modification date as if it were a legal review date.

## 5. Legal basis and sources

Where an article materially depends on legislation, identify the legal basis clearly.

Examples:
- Mediation (Civil and Commercial Disputes) Act, No. 13 of 2026
- Civil Procedure Code, where directly relevant
- other statutes or rules where they materially support the explanation

Do not overload ordinary-reader articles with citations merely to appear authoritative. Sources should improve transparency, not decoration.

## 6. Distinguish the type of statement

Where helpful, distinguish:
- statutory rule;
- procedural requirement;
- general legal principle;
- practical observation;
- educational mediation concept.

Avoid wording that turns a practical observation into an apparent rule of law.

## 7. Disclaimers

Use a concise and consistent disclaimer. The standard form is:

> General information only. This material is intended for legal information and legal literacy. It is not legal advice for any particular matter. The appropriate course depends on the facts, documents and applicable law.

A more specific disclaimer may be used where necessary.

## 8. Contact and conversion

Contact information may be provided neutrally as part of the website's professional identity.

Do not design trust signals around conversion language such as:
- "Book now";
- "Get results";
- "Winning representation";
- "Contact me before it is too late";
- or similar client-acquisition language.

## 9. Confidentiality

Do not use client names, outcomes, confidential facts or identifiable case details as credibility material unless disclosure is clearly lawful, professionally proper and expressly authorised where required.

## 10. Principle

Authority on this website should come from the quality, accuracy, clarity and transparency of the information — not from promotional claims.
