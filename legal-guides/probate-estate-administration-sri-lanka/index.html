Probate, Letters of Administration and Certificates of Heirship in Sri Lanka: What Is the Difference?

Three important legal documents explained in everyday language.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 6 November 2026Sri Lanka

Three important legal documents explained in everyday language.

What Is Probate?

Probate is a court grant associated with a deceased person's valid last will. Where the will appoints an executor, the executor may apply to the appropriate District Court to have it proved and receive authority to administer the estate.

For example, if a mother leaves a house to her daughter, the executor may need probate before the property can be dealt with through the required legal process.

What Are Letters of Administration?

Letters of administration authorise a person, called the administrator, to deal with a deceased person's estate. They are commonly obtained where no will exists. Letters may also be granted with the will annexed if a will exists but a suitable person other than an executor obtaining probate must act.

The administrator identifies assets, deals with valid debts and distributes property according to law. The grant does not make the administrator the sole beneficial owner.

What Is a Certificate of Heirship?

A certificate of heirship is a court-issued document identifying heirs. In appropriate intestate cases, certificates may identify heirs and their agreed shares. They are not a substitute for active estate administration in every case; the court may decide letters of administration are necessary.

Which Court Should the Family Approach?

These applications generally come before the appropriate District Court. Jurisdiction depends on statutory connections such as the deceased's residence, the applicant's residence or the location of estate land.

Does the Value of the Estate Matter?

Yes. The Civil Procedure Code contains requirements for certain intestate estates valued at Rs. 4 million or more. Section 525 imposes a reporting duty on the surviving spouse or next of kin where a person dies in Sri Lanka without a will and leaves property in Sri Lanka meeting the statutory threshold.

But the threshold does not mean every smaller estate is free from court procedure or transfer documentation.

What If Someone Objects?

Interested persons may object to an application through the prescribed process, for example disputing a will or the applicant's entitlement to administer the estate. The court may need to resolve disputed facts before issuing the grant.

Can Inherited Property Be Sold Immediately?

Not always. Section 545 of the Civil Procedure Code restricts certain transfers. The relevant period, value, existence of a will and available court documents can affect whether a transfer is permitted. Obtain title and succession advice before selling inherited land.

Conclusion

Probate, administration and certificates of heirship have different purposes. Establish whether a will exists, identify the estate and heirs, and then determine the correct procedure.

Legal References

  • Civil Procedure Code, sections 516–545
