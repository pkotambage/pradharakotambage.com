I Have a Cheque That Is One or Two Years Old – Is It Still Useful?

An old cheque may no longer be useful for ordinary bank presentation, but that does not necessarily mean the debt behind it has disappeared.

By Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 10 September 2026Short guide

A client sometimes produces a cheque and says:

“I have had this for more than a year. Can I still use it?”

The answer requires an important distinction.

An old cheque may no longer be useful for ordinary bank presentation, but that does not necessarily mean that the debt behind the cheque has disappeared.

Can you simply deposit an old cheque?

Usually, this is the first practical problem.

Banks may decline to honour a cheque when a considerable period has passed since the date written on it. For example, some Sri Lankan banking terms specifically provide that a bank may decline a cheque dated six months or more before presentation.

So if you are holding a cheque dated one or two years ago, do not assume that you can simply deposit it now and receive payment.

But that is not necessarily the end of the matter.

The cheque and the underlying debt are not always the same question

Suppose someone owed you Rs. 500,000 and gave you a cheque for that amount.

If the cheque later becomes too old for ordinary banking purposes, an important question remains:

Why was that cheque issued in the first place?

There may still be evidence of an underlying loan, sale, service, settlement or other obligation.

The fact that the cheque itself is old does not automatically establish that the person no longer owes you anything.

The old cheque itself may still be important evidence

Even where you cannot simply present the cheque through the bank, do not throw it away.

The cheque may still be relevant when considering:

  • who issued it;
  • the amount stated;
  • the date;
  • the transaction connected with it; and
  • whether there is other evidence of the debt.

WhatsApp messages, bank records, agreements, invoices or later acknowledgments may also become important in understanding why the cheque was given.

But do not keep waiting

An old cheque can also be a warning sign that too much time has already passed.

Sri Lankan law contains prescription periods for legal claims. Importantly, the period applicable to an action on the cheque itself may not necessarily be the same as the period applicable to the transaction or debt that existed behind it.

Therefore, if you are already holding a cheque that is one or two years old, it is sensible to examine the matter now rather than continue waiting for further promises of payment.

So, is an old cheque still useful?

Potentially, yes.

But its usefulness may no longer simply be:

“Can I deposit this cheque?”

The better questions may be:

“Why was this cheque given?”

“What other evidence do I have?”

and

“Am I still within the time allowed to take legal action?”

So if you find yourself holding an old cheque, preserve it and examine the transaction behind it before assuming either that the cheque is worthless or that you can simply present it whenever you wish.
