How to Obtain Letters of Administration in Sri Lanka: A Step-by-Step Guide

A practical court-procedure guide for families dealing with an estate without a will.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 6 November 2026Sri Lanka

A father dies without leaving a last will. He owned a house, a vehicle and some money in a bank account. His wife and children know that they may be entitled to inherit his property, but they are uncertain how to deal with the estate legally. In such circumstances, the family may need letters of administration issued by the appropriate District Court.

What Are Letters of Administration?

A father dies without a will, leaving a house, vehicle and bank account. His family may need letters of administration: a court grant authorising an administrator to deal with the estate. The administrator gathers assets, deals with debts and distributes the remainder according to law. The grant does not mean that the administrator inherits everything.

The administrator is a representative of the estate, not the person entitled to take every asset. They must deal with the property and liabilities in accordance with the court grant and law.

Step 1: Identify the Legal Heirs

Find out whether the surviving spouse, children, parents or other relatives are entitled to inherit. Determine whether general law or a special system such as Kandyan law, Thesawalamai or Muslim law governs. Collect relevant birth, marriage, death and adoption records.

Step 2: Identify Property and Debts

Prepare accurate details of land, buildings, bank balances, vehicles, investments and other assets, together with mortgages, loans and creditors. This information is needed for the application and administration.

Step 3: Determine Who Should Apply

Section 526 of the Civil Procedure Code permits an interested person to seek letters of administration. In competing claims, section 523 gives preference to the surviving widow or widower, although the court may depart from it for good cause. A widow appointed administrator does not thereby extinguish her children's inheritance rights.

For example, a widow may obtain administration even though her three adult children also have inheritance rights. These are separate legal questions.

Step 4: Check the Estate's Value

Section 525 contains a reporting duty where a person dies in Sri Lanka without a will leaving property in Sri Lanka valued at Rs. 4 million or more, subject to the section's conditions. The surviving spouse or next of kin must report the death within one month. Section 527 also addresses administration of such estates.

Do not assume that a smaller estate never needs a court document or that its land may be transferred without considering succession rules.

Step 5: Prepare the Court Application

Section 528 provides for a petition and affidavit setting out the death, absence of a will, heirs, estate property, liabilities, creditors, applicant's entitlement and agreed shares where relevant. The section specifies three months from the date of death for making the application. Seek advice if that period has passed.

Where shares are agreed and relevant, the application should include those details. Failure to disclose relevant heirs or creditors may give rise to procedural difficulties.

Step 6: Notify Other Interested Persons

The relevant next of kin must be identified. Written consents may be obtained, and the prescribed procedure for notifying non-consenting respondents must be followed. Concealing another legal heir can create serious complications.

Step 7: Publish the Notice

Section 529 requires publication on two separate occasions in consecutive months and provides an opportunity for objections. If unopposed and the evidence is sufficient, the court may make an appropriate order under section 531. Disputes may require an inquiry.

Step 8: Obtain the Grant

Under section 539, after the order the administrator takes the prescribed oath within fifteen days and files an inventory and valuation within one month after that oath. Letters are issued under the statutory procedure.

Step 9: Administer and Distribute

The administrator protects property, addresses valid liabilities and distributes the balance to entitled heirs while observing accounting duties. The estate is not the administrator's personal property.

Can the Family Obtain Certificates of Heirship Instead?

Sections 526 and 531 permit eligible heirs to seek certificates identifying heirs and their agreed shares in appropriate intestate cases. However, the court may determine that active administration and letters are necessary instead.

These certificates may help heirs prove their entitlement to particular inherited interests. But where active administration is required, certificates alone may not be sufficient.

What If There Are Debts or Disputes?

Valid creditors must be considered before distribution. Insolvent estates may involve separate statutory provisions. Heirs may object through the prescribed process; lawful negotiation may resolve practical differences but cannot replace required court or land-transfer formalities.

The Civil Procedure Code has provisions for administration of insolvent testamentary estates. Agreements among heirs cannot remove valid creditor rights or compulsory transfer requirements.

Can Heirs Sell the Property?

Section 545 restricts certain estate-property transfers within a specified five-year period. The date of death, estate value, existence of a will, form of grant and nature of the transaction can matter. Have the title and required documents examined before sale.

Conclusion

Letters of administration provide a lawful means of dealing with an intestate estate. Identifying heirs and assets, filing properly, notifying interested persons and following the court process helps protect everyone concerned.
