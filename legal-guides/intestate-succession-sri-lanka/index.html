Who Inherits Property When Someone Dies Without a Will in Sri Lanka?

Understand the shares of a surviving spouse, children and other family members under the general law.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 6 November 2026Sri Lanka

Understand the shares of a surviving spouse, children and other family members under the general law.

What Is Intestate Succession?

If a person dies without a legally effective will disposing of property, the applicable law determines who inherits it. This is called intestate succession. In Sri Lanka, the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance governs general-law intestacy, but different rules may apply under Kandyan law, Thesawalamai or Muslim law.

How Much Does the Surviving Husband or Wife Inherit?

Under section 22 of the Matrimonial Rights and Inheritance Ordinance, a surviving spouse ordinarily inherits one-half of the deceased spouse's intestate property. The other half passes to the remaining legal heirs under the statutory rules.

Example: A father dies leaving a wife, two children and a net distributable estate worth Rs. 12 million. Assuming general law applies:

Family memberShareValue
Wife1/2Rs. 6 million
First child1/4Rs. 3 million
Second child1/4Rs. 3 million

The same general principle applies to a husband surviving his wife. The calculation concerns the deceased person's property, not the surviving spouse's separately owned property.

Do Sons and Daughters Inherit Equally?

Under general law, children ordinarily inherit equally. The eldest son does not automatically receive more, and a married daughter does not lose her general-law inheritance rights because of marriage. If a mother dies leaving three children and no spouse, the children ordinarily receive one-third each. Adoption, parentage and special-law questions can require separate examination.

What If One Child Died Before the Parent?

Grandchildren may inherit the share that their deceased parent would have received. Suppose a grandmother had two children, one alive and the other deceased leaving two children. If the grandmother leaves no surviving spouse, the living child ordinarily receives half, and the two grandchildren share the other half. This is called inheritance by representation.

What If the Deceased Had No Children?

Parents may inherit where there are no descendants; siblings may also inherit in certain circumstances. If a person leaves a spouse, no descendants and two surviving parents, the spouse ordinarily receives one-half, with the parents sharing the remainder. The rules are more detailed if only one parent survives or there are full and half-siblings.

What If Only a Husband or Wife Survives?

Having no children does not automatically mean the surviving spouse receives everything. Parents, siblings or other qualifying relatives may have rights. If the relevant classes of other heirs fail, the surviving spouse may become entitled to the entire estate under the general law.

Can an Heir Sell the Inherited House?

Not necessarily. Several heirs may own undivided shares in one house or plot of land. This does not automatically divide the property into physical portions. One heir cannot ordinarily sell the entire property simply because they inherited a share; title and testamentary requirements must be checked.

What Should the Family Do?

Identify all potential heirs, inspect deeds and family records, establish the applicable law and consider whether a court grant or certificate is necessary. Where there are debts or disagreements about shares, obtain advice before distributing or transferring property.

Conclusion

Under Sri Lanka's general law, a surviving spouse ordinarily receives one-half of the intestate estate. Children and other relatives inherit according to the statutory order. The precise result depends on the family relationships, property and law applicable to the deceased.
