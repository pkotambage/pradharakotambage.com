Foreign Divorce Recognition

How to Get a Foreign Divorce Recognised in Sri Lanka

Married in Sri Lanka but divorced overseas? Understand how to have your foreign divorce legally recognised in Sri Lanka.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 4 November 2026Sri Lanka

You got married in Sri Lanka.

Later, you and your spouse moved to another country. Perhaps it was Canada, Australia or the United Kingdom.

Unfortunately, your marriage came to an end, and you obtained a divorce through a court in that country.

As far as the foreign court is concerned, your marriage has ended.

But now you have an important question:

Is your overseas divorce legally recognised in Sri Lanka?

This question becomes particularly important if you intend to remarry, return to Sri Lanka or deal with legal matters involving your marital status.

Sri Lankan law now provides a specific procedure for recognising qualifying foreign divorce judgments.

Here is a practical explanation of how the process works.

1. Is a Foreign Divorce Automatically Recognised in Sri Lanka?

Not necessarily.

Obtaining a divorce in another country does not automatically mean that it will be recognised in Sri Lanka under the applicable legal framework.

The Reciprocal Recognition, Registration and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments Act, No. 49 of 2024, which came into operation on 26 March 2025, provides a process for obtaining formal recognition of qualifying foreign divorce judgments.

This is important because you may not need to obtain another divorce in Sri Lanka if you already have a qualifying divorce judgment from a foreign court.

Instead, you may be able to apply to a Sri Lankan court to recognise the divorce that has already been granted overseas.

However, the foreign judgment must satisfy the legal requirements.

2. Which Countries' Divorce Judgments Can Be Recognised?

The Act applies to judgments delivered by courts of countries officially designated by the Sri Lankan Government.

Gazette Extraordinary No. 2429/51 identified 53 countries, including:

  • Canada
  • Australia
  • United Kingdom
  • New Zealand
  • India
  • Singapore
  • Malaysia
  • Maldives

If you obtained your divorce in one of these countries, you may be eligible to apply for recognition under the Act.

However, the country alone is not enough. Your marriage and divorce must also satisfy the relevant conditions.

If your divorce was granted in a country that does not appear in the applicable Gazette Orders, such as the United States under the March 2025 list, your position requires separate legal assessment.

3. What Conditions Must Your Foreign Divorce Satisfy?

The Act specifically addresses qualifying foreign divorce judgments concerning marriages registered under Sri Lanka's Marriage Registration Ordinance.

The foreign judgment must be final and conclusive.

In addition, at least one of the following conditions must be satisfied:

1. Domicile

Either spouse was legally domiciled in the country where the divorce was granted at the date of judgment.

Domicile is a legal concept and is not always the same as simply living somewhere.

2. Residence

Either spouse had been habitually resident in that country for at least one year immediately before the divorce judgment.

3. Nationality

Either spouse was a national of that country when the judgment was delivered.

4. Acceptance of the Foreign Court's Jurisdiction

Both spouses submitted to the jurisdiction of the foreign court.

You do not have to satisfy all four conditions. One may be sufficient, provided the other legal requirements are met.

Example

Nimal and Amali married in Sri Lanka in 2016.

They moved to Australia in 2019.

In 2024, an Australian court granted their divorce.

Because Australia is included in the designated countries and the couple had lived there for several years, the residence condition may support an application for recognition.

However, the final judgment and the other statutory requirements must still be examined.

4. How Do You Apply for Recognition in Sri Lanka?

The process generally involves five steps.

Step 1: Obtain Your Final Divorce Judgment

First, obtain the final divorce judgment from the foreign court.

Some countries issue an initial or provisional divorce order before the divorce becomes final.

A provisional order should not be confused with the final divorce judgment.

The Sri Lankan Act also requires a certificate issued by the relevant authority of the foreign court authenticating the judgment.

Step 2: Gather the Supporting Documents

Depending on your circumstances, relevant documents may include:

  • Your final foreign divorce judgment and the required authenticating certificate.
  • Your Sri Lankan marriage certificate.
  • Relevant passport or identification documents.
  • Documents establishing residence, domicile or nationality, where necessary.
  • Records concerning the other spouse's participation in the foreign proceedings.
  • Any translations required under Sri Lankan law.

The exact documents required must be considered according to the Act and the applicable court rules.

Step 3: File an Application in Sri Lanka

The application may be made to:

  • The District Court of Colombo; or
  • The District Court within whose local jurisdiction either spouse resides.

The application is supported by the necessary documents and affidavits.

The purpose is to obtain legal recognition of the foreign divorce, not to conduct the original divorce proceedings again.

Step 4: The Court Examines the Application

The court considers whether the foreign divorce meets the requirements of the Act.

The procedure also depends on whether the foreign judgment was delivered between the parties or obtained without the other spouse participating.

Step 5: Obtain the Court's Declaration

Where the legal requirements are satisfied, the Sri Lankan court can pronounce a declaration recognising the foreign divorce.

The declaration gives the foreign divorce the legal effect specified by the Act in Sri Lanka.

The Registrar of the court must also send a certified copy of the declaration to the Registrar-General for the purposes of the Marriage Registration Ordinance.

5. What If Your Former Spouse Did Not Participate in the Foreign Divorce?

This is a common concern.

Suppose you obtained a divorce in Canada, but your former spouse did not appear in the Canadian proceedings.

Does this prevent recognition?

Not necessarily.

Where the foreign divorce judgment was obtained ex parte, meaning without the other party participating in the proceedings, the Sri Lankan court must issue notice to the other spouse.

The other spouse then has an opportunity to raise an objection on legally permitted grounds.

These grounds include failure to satisfy the statutory application requirements, fraud or relevant misrepresentation.

If notice has been properly served and no objection is made within the specified period, the court may recognise the foreign divorce.

Therefore, an ex parte divorce is not automatically excluded.

However, the service of notice and the other procedural requirements must be complied with.

6. Can You Apply While Living in Canada, Australia or Another Country?

Many people who need a foreign divorce recognised in Sri Lanka are still living overseas.

For example, you may have obtained a divorce in Canada and intend to remain there permanently.

The Act allows an application to be made to the District Court of Colombo.

The initial recognition procedure is based on petitions, affidavits and other documents rather than oral evidence.

However, the law does not mean that physical attendance can never become necessary.

Arrangements concerning signing documents overseas, legal representation and any required attendance should be checked according to the circumstances of your case.

If you live abroad, it is sensible to clarify these requirements before arranging travel to Sri Lanka.

7. What If You Obtained Your Divorce Before 2025?

The new law came into operation on 26 March 2025.

However, that does not mean only divorces granted after that date can be recognised.

The Act expressly contemplates qualifying foreign divorce judgments entered before its commencement.

For example, if you obtained your divorce in Australia in 2022, you may still be eligible to seek recognition.

Generally, the application must be made within ten years from the date of the final divorce judgment.

The court may consider a later application if valid reasons for the delay are established.

A separate issue arises where recognition proceedings had already been commenced in Sri Lanka before the new law came into operation. Such proceedings may require consideration under the applicable earlier law and transitional provisions.

8. How Long Does It Take?

The Act directs the court to conclude the initial application proceedings within six months.

However, this is not a guaranteed completion period for every case.

The time involved may depend on the documents, service of notice, objections and any subsequent proceedings.

It is therefore sensible to begin the process in advance, particularly if you require recognition for an intended marriage or another important legal matter.

9. Can You Remarry in Sri Lanka After Getting Divorced Overseas?

Suppose you obtained a final divorce in Australia and now wish to marry someone in Sri Lanka.

Can you simply produce your Australian divorce judgment?

You should not assume that a foreign divorce document alone will be accepted for every legal purpose in Sri Lanka.

Where the statutory recognition procedure applies, a declaration recognising the foreign divorce establishes its legal effect in Sri Lanka under the Act.

Before arranging a new marriage, you should ensure that your earlier marriage has been legally dissolved and that the relevant Sri Lankan marriage registration requirements are satisfied.

This is particularly important because complications concerning marital status can have serious legal consequences.

10. Does Recognition of the Divorce Also Settle Property and Child Custody Matters?

Not automatically.

A foreign divorce judgment may deal with several matters at the same time.

For example, it might contain orders concerning:

  • The dissolution of the marriage.
  • Division of matrimonial property.
  • Financial support.
  • Custody or maintenance of children.

However, Act No. 49 of 2024 expressly excludes certain categories of foreign judgments, including matrimonial property settlements and specified orders relating to the custody, guardianship and maintenance of minors.

Consequently, recognition of the divorce itself should not be confused with recognition or enforcement of every related order.

These matters may require separate legal consideration.

11. What Should You Check Before Applying?

Before beginning the process, it is useful to establish:

Is your divorce final?

Ensure that you have the final judgment, not merely a provisional order.

Where was your divorce granted?

Check whether the country is covered by the applicable Gazette Orders.

Under which Sri Lankan law was your marriage registered?

The Act specifically addresses marriages registered under the Marriage Registration Ordinance. Different legal considerations may arise for Kandyan, Muslim or other marriages.

Did your former spouse participate in the foreign proceedings?

This may affect whether notice must be issued in Sri Lanka.

When was the divorce granted?

The date is relevant to the general ten-year application period.

Do you have the necessary documents?

Obtaining the appropriate records from the foreign court can help avoid delays.

Conclusion

For many Sri Lankans living overseas, obtaining a foreign divorce is an important step in moving forward with their lives.

However, when legal matters arise in Sri Lanka, the effect of that overseas divorce may need to be formally established.

The Reciprocal Recognition, Registration and Enforcement of Foreign Judgments Act, No. 49 of 2024 provides a procedure through which qualifying foreign divorce judgments may be recognised.

If you have already obtained a divorce in another country, you may not need to start another divorce case in Sri Lanka. You may instead be able to seek recognition of the foreign judgment.

The correct procedure depends on the country, the type of marriage, the nature of the divorce judgment and your individual circumstances.

Understanding these requirements before making an application can help you make informed decisions and avoid unnecessary complications.
