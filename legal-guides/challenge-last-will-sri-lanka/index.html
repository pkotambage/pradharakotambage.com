Can a Last Will Be Challenged in Court in Sri Lanka?

Unfairness alone is not enough. Understand the grounds and evidence that matter.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 6 November 2026Sri Lanka

A father dies leaving most of his property to one child. The other children believe the arrangement is unfair. One claims the father was seriously ill when the will was signed. Another suspects the beneficiary pressured him. Can the children challenge the will? Yes, but a will cannot be set aside merely because someone is unhappy with their inheritance. There must be a legally recognised reason.

Is an Unequal Inheritance a Reason to Challenge a Will?

A father dies leaving most of his property to one child. Others think this is unfair. Under the Wills (Amendment) Act, No. 29 of 2022, a person has broad freedom to decide who inherits, including excluding relatives. An unequal gift is not automatically unlawful. The real question is whether the will was legally valid.

What If the Person Lacked Mental Capacity?

A testator must have the required testamentary capacity when making the will. Serious illness or cognitive impairment can raise questions, but old age or physical illness alone does not establish incapacity. Medical evidence and testimony concerning the person's understanding at the time may matter.

The central question is the testator's actual understanding when the document was made. Witnesses, medical evidence and the surrounding circumstances may be relevant.

What If Someone Pressured the Deceased?

A will may be disputed if it resulted from legally impermissible threats or pressure. Ordinary affection, closeness or persuasion does not necessarily amount to undue influence. Evidence about what happened is essential.

What If the Signature Was Forged?

Fraud and forgery can undermine a will. Relevant evidence may include the original document, genuine signatures, notarial records and the testimony of attesting witnesses. An allegation is not proof.

What If the Will Was Not Properly Signed?

Mandatory signature, impression, notarial and witnessing requirements must be observed. The requirements in force at the time the will was executed must be identified.

What If Circumstances Look Suspicious?

If a major beneficiary organised the will, kept it secret or was unusually involved in execution, the circumstances may deserve scrutiny. Such involvement does not automatically prove invalidity; the evidence must be examined to establish genuine testamentary intentions.

For example, unusual secrecy or substantial involvement by a beneficiary may call for closer examination, but neither fact automatically proves a will invalid.

Who Can Challenge a Will?

A person ordinarily needs a sufficient legal interest in the estate, such as an heir whose rights would be affected or someone claiming under another will. Unrelated persons cannot generally object merely from disagreement.

What Evidence May Be Important?

  • The original will and earlier or later wills.
  • Notarial and witness evidence.
  • Relevant medical records.
  • Documents proving title.
  • Evidence of alleged pressure, fraud or forgery.
  • Relevant communications and surrounding circumstances.

When Should Objections Be Raised?

The Civil Procedure Code provides notice and objection procedures during probate. Section 536 provides for caveats in appropriate cases. A caveat is a formal procedural step by someone seeking to oppose a grant. Interested persons should act promptly.

Anyone with a genuine basis to contest the application should preserve documents and seek timely legal assistance instead of relying on informal accusations.

Can Probate Be Challenged After It Has Been Granted?

In specified circumstances, sections 537 and 538 allow applications to recall or revoke a grant. A completed probate cannot simply be reopened because a relative changes their mind; legal grounds and the prescribed procedure are required.

Can Mediation Help?

Family members may be able to negotiate practical issues such as occupation or sale of inherited property. Mediation cannot replace a court determination that a disputed will is valid or dispense with statutory steps. Any agreement must respect the rights of affected persons.

Possible practical arrangements may concern occupation of the family home, sale of jointly inherited land or distribution of sentimental belongings. A mediator cannot by agreement prove a forged instrument genuine or replace statutory probate procedure.

Conclusion

A will may be challenged for recognised reasons such as fraud, forgery, lack of capacity, undue influence or improper execution—not merely because the distribution seems unfair.

Legal References

  • Wills Ordinance, as amended by Act No. 29 of 2022
  • Prevention of Frauds Ordinance
  • Evidence Ordinance
  • Civil Procedure Code, sections 529, 532, 536–538 and 541
