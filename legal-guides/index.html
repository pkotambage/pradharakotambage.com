Legal Guides

Start with the problem, not the legal category.

These guides are written around the questions people actually face — a letter that has arrived, money that has not been paid, a case that has been filed, a workplace problem, or a family-law question.

Understand what the law means, what usually happens next, and the important questions to consider.

Written for ordinary readers

The aim is plain, practical explanation. Legal terminology is used where it matters, but the starting point is always the real-life situation rather than the name of a statute or branch of law.

General information, not individual advice

Every dispute turns on its own facts, documents, dates and applicable law. These guides are intended to improve legal understanding; they do not determine what any particular person should do in a specific matter.

A note about legal information

The law may change, and the result of a legal problem can depend on facts that a general guide cannot address. Where a guide refers to legislation, procedure or an important legal rule, the aim will be to identify the relevant source and keep the material under review.
