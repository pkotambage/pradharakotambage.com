Family Law

Can Both Spouses Agree to Divorce in Sri Lanka?

Mutual consent, uncontested divorce, pleadings, evidence, children and financial claims explained.

Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-Law26 September 2026Sri Lanka
Englishසිංහල

1. Agreement is not itself a ground

Both spouses may conclude that the marriage should end without seeking confrontation or compensation. But section 19 of the Marriage Registration Ordinance does not make mutual consent a standalone ground: the recognised grounds under general marriage law are adultery after marriage, malicious desertion and incurable impotence at the time of marriage. An uncontested procedure does not change that legal requirement.

2. What does uncontested divorce mean?

An uncontested matrimonial action is one in which the defendant does not oppose the plaintiff’s claim for divorce. The parties may also agree on practical arrangements. But the absence of opposition does not amount to proof, and the court is not required to grant divorce merely because both spouses want it.

3. How might proceedings be conducted?

The plaintiff files a plaint stating a legally sustainable ground, material facts and relief sought. Depending on the circumstances, a duly served defendant may not file an answer; may communicate a non-contesting position through an appropriate affidavit; or may file an answer setting out their position and any relief properly available to them. An affidavit is not a universal substitute for procedural requirements. An answer seeking matrimonial relief must have its own legal basis. It is important to check every item of relief requested before choosing not to contest. The court can receive the evidence needed to determine the case without a prolonged contested trial.

4. Does one spouse have to be legally at fault?

The ordinary grounds under general marriage law are fault-based or depend on a specified condition at marriage. Even when both spouses prefer a peaceful separation, the pleaded ground must be genuine and proved. The parties’ agreement is different from a judicial finding. Neither spouse should fabricate allegations or testimony merely to obtain a decree. Sections 601 and 602 of the Civil Procedure Code also require scrutiny of proof and collusion.

5. What happens to damages and alimony?

Spouses may decide not to pursue particular financial claims and may reflect their positions in the plaint, answer or appropriate recorded terms. That does not mean every potential right disappears automatically. Sections 614 and 615 of the Civil Procedure Code address alimony and financial orders; the court’s powers and the position of children must be considered separately. A party should understand the effect of any concession or settlement.

6. What if there are minor children?

The parents may propose custody or shared arrangements, access and contact, maintenance, education and healthcare responsibilities. Sometimes one parent has custody and the other provides support and reasonable access. Agreements remain subject to the child’s welfare and the court’s powers; a divorce agreement cannot simply bargain away a child’s interests.

7. Can the court refuse an agreed divorce?

Yes. Pleadings must state a sustainable cause of action and evidence must establish it. If, for example, malicious desertion is pleaded but testimony does not prove its required elements or materially contradicts the plaint, the court may dismiss the claim. The court is not bound by a defendant’s silence or agreement. Collusion and false evidence are not lawful shortcuts.

8. When does the decree become final?

The court initially pronounces a decree nisi; under sections 604 and 605 the decree ordinarily cannot be made absolute before at least three months, or a longer period the court prescribes. For an ex parte decree, the relevant period runs from service of the decree nisi on the defaulting party. Final dissolution follows decree absolute.

9. Is there another route under the Code?

Section 608(2) provides a separate route following two years from a decree of separation, or seven years of separation a mensa et thoro without such a decree, subject to statutory conditions and proof. This is not the same as a divorce granted solely by mutual agreement.

10. A practical illustration

A couple may agree that the marriage should end, arrange their child’s care and support, and wish not to pursue financial claims. They may consider an uncontested action. Yet a real statutory ground must still be pleaded and proved, and the proposed arrangements may require appropriate treatment by the court. Agreement can narrow the dispute; it cannot replace the law.

11. Frequently asked questions

Can a signed agreement alone dissolve the marriage? No, not under general marriage law. Must a defendant always file an answer? Not necessarily; the proper course depends on the relief and procedural situation. Is evidence required when neither party contests? Yes. Can parents agree on custody and access? They may propose arrangements, subject to the child’s welfare and the court’s determination.
