Can WhatsApp Messages Be Used to Prove a Debt?

WhatsApp messages may become important evidence, but one convenient screenshot does not automatically prove that money is owed.

By Pradhara Kotambage, Attorney-at-LawPublished 10 September 2026Short guide

A common question in money recovery disputes is:

“I don’t have a written agreement, but I have all the WhatsApp messages. Is that enough?”

The answer is that WhatsApp messages may become important evidence, but having a screenshot does not automatically prove that money is owed.

What do the messages actually say?

Consider the difference between these two situations.

You transfer Rs. 500,000 to someone and later receive a message saying:

“I know I still owe you the Rs. 500,000. Give me another two months to pay.”

That may be very significant.

Compare it with a message simply saying:

“Don’t worry. I will settle everything soon.”

The second message may still be relevant, but important questions remain. What was being discussed? What does “everything” mean? What amount was involved?

This is why context matters.

Don’t keep only the convenient screenshot

If a dispute is developing, try to preserve the relevant WhatsApp conversation in its original form.

Do not keep only one screenshot that appears favourable to you.

The messages immediately before and after it may help establish:

  • who was discussing the transaction;
  • the amount involved;
  • why the money was paid;
  • whether the other person acknowledged receiving it;
  • whether repayment was promised; and
  • whether further time was requested.

A complete conversation can tell a much clearer story than an isolated screenshot.

Keep the original messages

If the WhatsApp conversation may later become important, avoid deleting the chat or relying entirely on screenshots forwarded to another phone.

Keep the original messages and, where possible, preserve a backup or export of the relevant conversation.

Questions about the authenticity, completeness and context of electronic material can become important if the contents are later disputed.

A bank transfer plus WhatsApp messages can tell a stronger story

A bank transfer by itself may establish that money moved from one person to another.

But it may not explain why.

Was it a loan? An investment? Payment for goods? A gift?

WhatsApp messages surrounding the transaction may help explain its true nature.

For example, a bank transfer together with later messages acknowledging the amount and promising repayment may provide a much clearer picture than either item considered alone.

What if the other person deletes the messages?

If you still have the conversation on your own device, preserve it carefully.

More importantly, if you believe a dispute is developing, do not wait until messages, phones or other records are lost before collecting the available evidence.

So, are WhatsApp messages enough?

Sometimes they may form an important part of proving a debt. In other cases, they may only support other evidence such as bank records, agreements, invoices, receipts or witness testimony.

The better question is therefore not simply:

“Do I have WhatsApp screenshots?”

It is:

“When these messages are read together with the rest of my evidence, what do they actually prove?”

Practical point: If someone owes you money, preserve the whole story, not merely the screenshot that appears most useful.
